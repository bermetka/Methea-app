## 2. The loop  [VISUAL: rewrite loop]
H2: Does this sound familiar?
You pick a topic you care about. You write a research question. Your supervisor says it’s “too broad”. You narrow it. Now it’s “not researchable”. You add a theory because the template says you need one. Then someone asks: “How does this theory shape your interview questions?” — and you realise it doesn’t.

So you start again.

One research methods instructor we interviewed told us her Master’s students spend 4–5 weeks and up to 8–9 rewrites just to define what they’re researching. Not collecting data. Not writing. Just figuring out what the study is.

If you’re stuck in that loop, it’s not because you’re a weak student. It’s because nobody teaches the part that matters most.

## 3. The vocabulary trap  [VISUAL later: 4 term cards]
H2: Four terms. Ten different definitions online.
Your template asks for a theoretical framework. A YouTube video talks about a conceptual framework. Your supervisor mentions an analytical framework. And half of what you’ve written so far is actually a literature review.

Here’s the short version nobody gives you:
- Literature review — what other studies found. It shows the gap.
- Theoretical framework — an existing theory you borrow as a lens. It tells you what to look at.
- Conceptual framework — your own map of how the key concepts in your study connect.
- Analytical framework — how you will turn your data into findings.

Knowing the definitions is the easy part. Making all four point in the same direction is where proposals fall apart.

## 4. The real reason proposals come back  [VISUAL: chain, MAIN visual]
H2: It’s not your writing. It’s the chain.
Every solid study is a chain:
question → theoretical lens → concepts → method → the questions you ask → how you analyse the answers.

Each link has to follow from the one before. When one link is off, everything after it is off too — and each part can still look fine on its own. That’s why the feedback feels vague: “it doesn’t hang together”, “where does the theory come in?”, “why interviews?”.

## 5. Why the usual fixes don’t work
H2: You’ve probably tried these already.
- Your supervisor — knows exactly what’s wrong, but has many students and very little time for each of them.
- YouTube and blogs — explain each term on its own. None of them look at your topic.
- ChatGPT — writes confident paragraphs that sound right and don’t connect. And many universities now flag AI-written text.

What’s missing is someone who walks through your study, link by link, and makes you explain each choice.

## 6. What it costs you to leave it
H2: The chain doesn’t break at the proposal. It breaks later.
A loose chain survives the proposal stage more often than you’d think. It breaks when you sit down with 15 interview transcripts and realise your questions didn’t ask about the things your theory says matter. Or at your defence, when someone asks “why this theory and not another?”

At that point, fixing it means redoing data collection. Fixing it now is a matter of hours.
